Abstract

Garment flattening supports downstream garment manipulation tasks such as folding and ironing. Existing value-based methods are limited by low-throughput physics, fixed-view low-fidelity rendering, and narrow asset coverage, making learned grasp values difficult to transfer to real garments with richer topology and visual variation. To address this, we develop a structural-to-visual value-learning framework spanning data generation, structural value modeling, and visual affordance distillation. First, we build an Isaac-based data pipeline that combines high-throughput physics simulation with randomized high-fidelity rendering over diverse garment assets. Second, we perform large-scale physics evaluation of dual-grasp pairs under standardized dual-arm unfolding conditions, and construct a Pair-Value Teacher to learn the structural pair-value distribution on 3D garment point clouds. Third, we project this structural value distribution onto randomized high-fidelity rendered observations and normalize it into dense image-space supervision for a directly deployable Visual Affordance Prior. Without fine-tuning on real data, our Visual Affordance Prior improves zero-shot transfer on aRTF-Clothes over ClothMate, increasing deformable/rigid Semantic Template AUC from 0.380/0.350 to 0.869/0.843. The same comparison on 22 real-robot garment assets shows higher final unfolding quality, with mean IoU increasing from 64.9 to 77.6 and coverage from 71.6 to 86.2.
